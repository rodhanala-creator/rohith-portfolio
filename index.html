ProjectOrchestrix AI
RoleAI automation engineer · product builder
TypeMulti-channel service operations platform
FocusLead handling, AI intake, routing, operator control

An AI operations platform built to handle enquiry capture, qualification, routing, and follow-up from one premium system.

Orchestrix AI was designed for service businesses that were growing faster than their internal processes. Enquiries were coming from multiple places, qualification was inconsistent, manual admin was eating time, and teams had no single view of what needed attention next. I approached the project as a real product build — not as a chatbot demo — and structured it around intake, automation, human review, and operational visibility.

The problem

In many service businesses, the first stage of the customer journey is where quality drops fastest. New leads arrive through forms, WhatsApp, or direct messages. Important information is missing, follow-up depends on who sees it first, and the handoff from conversation to real business action is rarely clean. The result is slower response, weaker conversion, and too much repeated admin.

  • Lead information arrived in inconsistent formats with missing context.
  • Manual qualification made response times slower and less reliable.
  • Teams lacked one dashboard for visibility across intake and follow-up.
  • Operators had no clean review layer to override or approve AI decisions.

My approach

I structured the platform around one central operating idea: every new enquiry should move through a clear product flow — capture, classify, route, review, and act. That turned a messy communication problem into a product system with visible states, defined actions, and a much calmer operator experience.

What this project shows

Product framing, AI-assisted workflow design, dashboard UX, event-driven system thinking, operator controls, and the ability to translate business friction into a buildable software product.

ProjectSignalDesk
RoleProduct engineer · dashboard designer
TypeAnalytics and reporting workspace
FocusPerformance visibility, client reporting, operational clarity

A reporting and analytics workspace built for teams that need clearer decisions, not more dashboard clutter.

SignalDesk was designed for agencies and service teams managing multiple clients, multiple data sources, and multiple stakeholders at once. The challenge was not a lack of data — it was the lack of a product layer that made the data useful. I built the concept around visibility, focus, and decision-ready reporting instead of generic dashboard noise.

The problem

Reporting systems often fail because everything gets treated as equally important. Important trend lines sit beside less useful metrics, client reporting feels detached from internal action, and teams end up exporting data just to make sense of it. The product needed to support both high-level summary and operational detail without becoming visually heavy.

  • Important metrics were buried inside flat dashboards and spreadsheets.
  • Client reporting and internal performance views were disconnected.
  • Different users needed different levels of access and detail.
  • Teams needed insight they could act on, not just data they could view.

My approach

I designed the product around layers: executive summary first, then account-level detail, then drill-down performance views. That created a cleaner hierarchy and made the system feel more like a serious analytics product than a collection of charts.

What this project shows

Dashboard architecture, data-heavy UX, role-aware interface structure, chart planning, reporting workflows, and product thinking around visibility and decision making.

ProjectFlowForge
RoleSystems product designer · full-stack builder
TypeWorkflow automation builder
FocusVisual automations, configurable infrastructure, approval logic

A visual workflow product built for businesses that had outgrown manual processes and fragile automation.

FlowForge was built around a common operational problem: businesses accumulate tasks, rules, approvals, delays, and cross-tool actions long before they build a proper workflow system. The result is messy internal processes, hidden dependencies, and automations that only one person understands. I designed this as a product that made workflow logic visible, editable, and reliable.

The problem

Teams were using a mixture of manual processes, disconnected tools, and ad-hoc scripts to keep work moving. That worked until complexity grew. Once approvals, retries, waits, notifications, and role-based actions entered the picture, there was no shared mental model of how the system actually behaved.

  • Workflows were difficult to understand and harder to change safely.
  • Important actions depended on memory or technical intervention.
  • Approvals and timed actions had no clean orchestration layer.
  • Visibility into execution history and failure states was weak.

My approach

I built the project around a visual builder model. Instead of burying workflow logic in backend code, the product exposed it through nodes and relationships — triggers, conditions, delays, approvals, and actions — while still mapping those steps to a real execution layer underneath.

What this project shows

Internal tool thinking, visual workflow UX, event-driven logic, system design, execution visibility, and advanced product structure for operations-heavy software.

ProjectHirePilot
RoleFull-stack product freelancer · workflow systems designer
TypeRecruitment operations platform
FocusCandidate scoring, hiring workflow, recruiter collaboration

An AI-assisted recruitment platform built to make hiring pipelines clearer, faster, and easier to manage.

HirePilot was created as a product for teams managing candidate flow across multiple roles and multiple decision-makers. Instead of treating recruitment as a stack of spreadsheets and forms, the product approached it as an operational software problem: intake, evaluation, progression, collaboration, and reporting all needed a clearer system.

The problem

Recruitment becomes messy very quickly once candidate volume increases. Notes scatter across tools, role context gets lost, recruiters work from different standards, and pipeline visibility weakens. AI can help, but only if it supports the recruiter rather than replacing judgement with opaque scoring.

  • Candidate information lacked consistency across roles and stages.
  • Shortlisting and review decisions were harder to compare.
  • Recruiter collaboration had too much hidden context.
  • Pipeline reporting and workload visibility were weak.

My approach

I structured the product around role-based pipelines, candidate records, scoring support, recruiter notes, and stage movement visibility. The system treated AI as an assistive layer for analysis and prioritisation while keeping the interface transparent and recruiter-led.

What this project shows

Advanced CRUD and workflow design, role-aware product structure, human-in-the-loop AI support, dashboard thinking, and the ability to build serious SaaS-style interfaces for operational products.

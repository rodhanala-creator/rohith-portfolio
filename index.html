FlowPilot — reframing daily service operations as one structured product.

Appointment-led businesses often run bookings, staff load, customer notes, reminders, and follow-ups across several disconnected tools. This project imagines a single product that reduces fragmentation and gives operators a more useful view of daily work, team capacity, and service delivery.

RoleProduct strategy, UX structure, interface design, front-end concept direction.
ScopeScheduling, team coordination, customer history, reminders, and daily operations visibility.
GoalCreate a calmer product experience than typical dashboard tools used by service businesses.
FramingPresented as a real-world operational platform rather than a generic SaaS template.
Challenge

Too many tools, not enough clarity.

The core problem was not missing software, but disconnected workflow. Owners and staff needed one view that could support the pace of a real working day instead of forcing them across multiple tabs and systems.

  • No single source of truth for bookings and client context.
  • Daily team balancing required too much manual checking.
  • Customer follow-through often fell behind operational work.
Approach

Design around the rhythm of the workday.

I mapped the product around real operational moments: opening, booking changes, active service windows, staff balancing, reminders, and end-of-day review. That sequence shaped the information hierarchy and feature grouping.

  • Glanceable panels over spreadsheet-style density.
  • Actions grouped by operational moment, not software category.
  • Customer detail available quickly without losing daily context.
Impact framing

A more believable service-business platform.

The final concept feels less like a SaaS starter template and more like a product designed for real operators. The interface prioritises pace, clarity, and calm decision-making over decorative complexity.

  • Sharper product proposition for service businesses.
  • Clearer scheduling and utilisation visibility.
  • More premium and credible presentation of operations software.

SignalDesk AI — turning enquiry chaos into a structured qualification system.

Local service businesses often lose opportunities because enquiries arrive through different channels and get handled inconsistently. This project explores an AI-assisted intake layer that captures intent, qualifies leads, routes urgent work, and gives teams clearer next actions.

RoleProduct concepting, workflow mapping, UX structure, interface design.
ScopeLead capture, intent detection, urgency scoring, routing, and operator review.
GoalPosition AI as operational infrastructure rather than trend-led decoration.
FramingDesigned to feel like a deployable business tool, not a chatbot concept page.
Problem

Response quality drops when triage is inconsistent.

Many small and mid-sized businesses have demand, but the process around first contact is weak. Enquiries are delayed, incomplete, or poorly prioritised, which hurts both conversion and internal efficiency.

  • Urgent leads can get buried with low-priority questions.
  • Teams repeat the same qualification work every day.
  • Different channels create fragmented context.
System logic

Trust comes from explainable workflow.

I designed the system around concrete signals: category, urgency, confidence, recommended action, and escalation path. The interface shows what the system inferred and why, making it more usable and more trustworthy.

Design principle

Human-in-the-loop by design.

The product does not try to replace people. It supports operators with structured intake, suggested next actions, and clean handoff points — an approach that aligns better with real service operations.

Even strong digital work can feel disorganised when approvals, files, updates, and communication are scattered across tools. This project imagines a dedicated client portal that improves trust, visibility, and overall delivery experience for freelancers and small studios.

RoleProduct direction, UX structure, interface design, content architecture.
ScopeOnboarding, project visibility, deliverables, approvals, feedback, and invoices.
GoalMake project delivery feel more premium and less fragmented for both team and client.
FramingPresented as a client-facing product layer, not just a project dashboard.
Context

Client experience is part of the product.

Delivery systems shape how clients perceive the quality of work. When the delivery layer is unclear, even strong outcomes can feel less professional. This project treats the delivery experience as a product in itself.

Make progress legible and approvals frictionless.

Every screen was designed to answer the same questions quickly: what is happening, what needs review, what changed, and what comes next.

Direction

LaunchLayer — helping lean teams move from “nearly ready” to actually launch-ready.

Early-stage teams often manage launches across docs, chats, and generic PM tools that were never designed around release confidence. This product focuses on launch readiness, dependency visibility, approvals, and blockers so teams can coordinate execution with less uncertainty.

RoleWorkflow design, product planning, UX/UI, information hierarchy.
ScopeReadiness tracking, dependency mapping, blocker visibility, and review cadence.
GoalCreate a coordination tool for startups without enterprise-level heaviness.
FramingBuilt as a product for launch execution rather than generic project management.
Insight

Launch management is a distinct workflow.

Teams do not just need tasks before launch. They need confidence, dependency awareness, sign-off visibility, and a clear sense of what could still go wrong.

Approach

Make risk visible without making the tool heavy.

The interface balances urgency with restraint by surfacing blockers and readiness signals clearly while staying compact enough for lean teams.

Positioning

Sharper than a broad PM tool.

A focused use case creates a more believable product story than another general dashboard. The specificity is what gives this project strength.

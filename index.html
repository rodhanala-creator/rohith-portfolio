FlowPilot

A service operations platform for appointment-led businesses that need clearer scheduling, team coordination, and customer follow-through without relying on scattered tools.

Role Product strategy, UX, UI, front-end system design
Project Business operations platform
Year 2026
View case study+

Reframing daily salon and service operations as one structured product.

Many appointment-driven businesses still manage bookings, staff allocation, reminders, notes, and customer follow-up across multiple disconnected tools. This concept imagines a unified operating layer that reduces fragmentation and gives owners a cleaner view of daily performance, team load, and service delivery.

1 shared workspace for scheduling, team capacity, customer records, and service follow-through
3 main user goals: keep the day on track, reduce manual admin, improve customer visibility
5 core product areas: bookings, availability, messaging, notes, and business overview
0 interest in decorative complexity — the product needed to feel calm, practical, and immediately usable
Challenge
Disconnected admin creates friction at every stage.

The biggest problem was not lack of tools, but lack of coherence. Booking systems, team notes, customer preferences, reminders, and revenue visibility often live in different places, which creates operational drag throughout the day.

  • No single source of truth for the team
  • Customer history is difficult to access quickly
  • Owners spend too much time stitching together daily operations
Approach
Design the product around the pace of the workday.

Instead of starting with features, I mapped the operating rhythm of a typical business day — opening, booking changes, walk-ins, staff balancing, reminders, and end-of-day review. That sequence became the structure of the interface.

  • Prioritised glanceable information over dense tables
  • Grouped actions by operational moment, not software category
  • Used compact panels to support fast decision-making
Outcome
A product concept that feels service-aware, not generic.

The final direction positions the platform as a calm operating system for service businesses. It is intentionally premium without feeling over-designed, helping it read more like a real market-ready product than a dashboard template.

  • Clear scheduling hierarchy
  • Practical customer detail access
  • More believable value proposition for real operators

Early structure focused on workflow sequencing.
Interface direction
Operational clarity with premium restraint.
Daily view

The UI language stays neutral and highly legible, with colour used sparingly to support operational meaning rather than decoration. This keeps the product closer to a premium business tool than a generic SaaS concept.

SignalDesk AI

An AI-assisted intake and lead-routing system designed for local service businesses that need faster response handling, cleaner qualification, and less manual triage.

Role Product concept, system mapping, UX structure, interface design
Project AI operations and intake workflow
Year 2026
View case study+

Turning first-contact chaos into a structured qualification system.

Service businesses often lose opportunities not because they lack demand, but because enquiries arrive through too many channels and are handled inconsistently. This product concept explores how AI-assisted intake can capture intent, qualify leads, and escalate high-value conversations faster.

4 input sources considered: web forms, WhatsApp, direct messages, and callback requests
1 main objective: reduce friction between first enquiry and next business action
3 decision states used: qualified, needs follow-up, escalate now
24/7 product positioning designed around continuous capture rather than office-hour dependency
Problem framing
Enquiries are valuable, but the process around them is often weak.

Businesses need a way to respond quickly without creating more admin. The opportunity here was to design an interface that feels less like a chatbot dashboard and more like a decision layer for inbound work.

  • Lead quality is inconsistent
  • Urgent enquiries are easy to miss
  • Manual triage slows down response time
System thinking
A better workflow depends on confidence, not just automation.

I designed the product around trust-building touchpoints: what the system captured, how it scored urgency, when it should escalate to a human, and how teams review the interaction history. That prevents the product from feeling like black-box automation.

Flow modelQualification path



Decision logicEscalation rules



Interface system
Built to explain what the AI is doing.

The interface avoids abstract AI language and instead presents concrete operational signals: intent, urgency, category, confidence, next step, and owner. This makes the product easier to trust and easier to act on.

Solution detail
Human-in-the-loop by design.

Rather than replacing people, the system supports them with structured intake, suggested actions, and clear routing. That framing makes it more believable for small business adoption and more aligned with real operations.

ClientPort

A premium client collaboration portal for freelancers and studios that need a more polished way to manage approvals, files, project visibility, and stakeholder communication.

Role Product direction, experience design, interface system
Project Client experience platform
Year 2026
View case study+

Designing a calmer, more premium client experience than email threads and shared folders.

For freelancers and small studios, delivery often becomes harder as projects grow. Feedback gets fragmented, approvals become unclear, and clients lose visibility. This concept imagines a dedicated portal that makes the relationship feel more structured, transparent, and high-trust.

1 central place for tasks, files, approvals, updates, and communication
2 primary audiences: delivery team and client stakeholders
5 core modules: onboarding, timeline, deliverables, approvals, invoices
High visual emphasis on trust, clarity, and premium client perception
Business context
Client experience is part of the product.

Even strong creative or development work can feel disorganised if the client experience around it is weak. This project treats the delivery layer as a product in itself.

Design goal
Make progress legible and approvals frictionless.

The experience needed to reduce ambiguity. Every screen was designed to answer a client’s likely questions quickly: what is happening, what needs me, what changed, and what comes next.

Visual direction
Softly premium, not luxury for the sake of it.

The interface uses restrained contrast, strong spacing, and clean hierarchy so the product feels elevated without sacrificing function.

LaunchLayer

A launch-readiness workspace for startup teams managing milestones, cross-functional dependencies, and execution risk before release.

Role Product planning, workflow design, UX, interface direction
Project Team coordination and delivery tool
Year 2026
View case study+

Helping lean teams move from “nearly ready” to actually launch-ready.

Early-stage teams often manage launches across documents, chats, and project tools that were never designed around launch risk. This concept focuses specifically on release coordination, readiness signals, and blocker visibility so execution becomes easier to trust.

6 key launch areas considered: product, content, QA, legal, support, and stakeholder sign-off
1 clear readiness score replaces vague “almost done” thinking
Lean designed for startups that do not need heavyweight PM software
Fast visual hierarchy optimised for weekly review and launch standups
Insight
Launch management is a distinct workflow, not a generic task board.

I approached this product by identifying what teams really need before launch: confidence, visibility, accountability, and a clear sense of what could still go wrong. That created a sharper product proposition than another broad PM interface.

Design principle
Make risk visible without making the tool feel heavy.

The interface balances urgency with restraint. It highlights dependency and risk clearly but avoids enterprise-style density, keeping it suitable for founder-led or lean operating teams.

Module 1
Readiness overview

A fast-glance surface showing launch confidence, pending approvals, and key blockers.

Module 2
Dependency map

A clearer way to understand which workstreams unlock others and where delays will cascade.

Module 3
Review cadence

Weekly launch review flow designed to support action, not just status reporting.
